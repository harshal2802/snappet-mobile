SnappetAI — Privacy Policy

Effective date: June 15, 2026 · Last updated: November 22, 2026

SnappetAI ("the app", "we", "us") is built around a simple principle: your data stays on your device, or in cloud storage that belongs to you. This policy explains what the app accesses, why, and what we do — and do not — do with it.

The short version

  • No SnappetAI account. No sign-up. No servers. SnappetAI has no backend. We do not have a server that receives, stores, or processes your data.
  • Everything is processed and stored locally on your iPhone and Apple Watch. Your workouts, heart-rate data, photos, videos, highlight reels, journal entries, habits, expenses, and budgets leave your device only when you export or share them, or when you turn on one of the optional backups to your own iCloud or Google Drive (described below). Both are off until you switch them on.
  • Signing in with Google is optional and is used only for those Google Drive features. The app works fully without it.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising, and no data-collecting third-party SDKs. (The app bundles an on-device machine-learning runtime to generate climbs; it makes no network calls and collects nothing.)
  • We never sell, rent, or share your data with anyone.

Data the app accesses (and why)

All access is requested just-in-time, with the system permission prompt, and is used only on your device for the feature you invoked:

  • Health data (Apple HealthKit) — with your permission, the app reads heart-rate, resting heart-rate, and workout data to build heart-rate-driven highlight reels, show your workout summary, and estimate effort/energy. On Apple Watch, the app may write a workout session so heart rate keeps streaming during a workout. HealthKit data is processed on your device and is never used for advertising or marketing, nor shared or sold to any third party. It leaves your device in one case only: if you turn on Automatic backup and switch on "Include heart-rate samples", the heart-rate samples saved with your workouts are included in the encrypted backup in your own cloud storage. That switch is off by default.
  • Photos & videos — with your permission, the app reads photos/videos shot during a workout to assemble highlight reels, and can save a finished reel back to your library. Media is read and rendered on-device. Nothing is uploaded unless you switch on "Keep clip copies in Google Drive" (below).
  • Camera — used only if you scan a receipt in the expense tools. Images are processed on-device (text recognition) and are not uploaded.
  • Bluetooth — used only to connect to accessories you choose: a heart-rate monitor or chest strap for live heart rate, and a climbing board or training device you connect in the app.
  • Local network — used only if you share a household in the Household app, to find the other phones in that household on the same Wi-Fi (see below).
  • Notifications — used only for workout/rest and focus-timer alerts you start.

What leaves your device — only when you choose

  • Sharing or exporting a reel, workout, or other content via the iOS share sheet (Photos, AirDrop, Messages, …). What happens then is governed by the destination you pick.
  • Automatic backup (optional, off by default). If you turn it on in Apps → Cloud & Backup, SnappetAI keeps a copy of your records (workouts, climbing, journal, habits, finance, and the rest of the app's database) in your own iCloud Drive. The copy is encrypted on your iPhone before it leaves, with a key stored in your keychain; we cannot read it and it is never sent to us. Heart-rate samples are left out unless you switch on "Include heart-rate samples". Photos and videos are not part of it.
  • Automatic backup to Google Drive (optional). The same encrypted backup can go to your Google Drive instead of iCloud. It is stored in a private app folder that only SnappetAI can read; SnappetAI cannot see your other Drive files.
  • Clip copies in Google Drive (optional, off by default). If you switch on "Keep clip copies in Google Drive", SnappetAI copies your session photos and videos to a "Snappet Clips" folder in your own Google Drive, and can play or bring back a clip from there after it has been removed from your iPhone. These are ordinary files you can see and delete in Drive; they are not encrypted by SnappetAI.
  • iCloud device backup, if you have it enabled for the device, may include the app's local data — this is Apple's encrypted backup, governed by Apple's privacy policy.

Google sign-in and Google user data

Signing in with Google is optional and happens in the system browser sheet; SnappetAI never sees your Google password. When you sign in, SnappetAI asks for only the following, and uses each only for the feature named:

  • Your email address — shown in the app so you can see which account is connected. It is stored on your iPhone and sent nowhere else.
  • A private app folder in your Google Drive — to store and read back the encrypted Automatic backup.
  • Files SnappetAI itself creates in your Google Drive — the "Snappet Clips" folder and the clip copies in it. SnappetAI cannot see or change any other file in your Drive.
  • Items you choose in Google Photos — only if you use "Pick from Google Photos" to find footage that is no longer on your iPhone. SnappetAI reads only the photos and videos you select in Google's own picker, and copies those that match a missing clip into your "Snappet Clips" folder. It has no access to the rest of your Google Photos library.

Where it goes. Google user data travels only between your iPhone and Google. It is never sent to us or to anyone else: there is no SnappetAI server. Your sign-in tokens are kept in the keychain on your iPhone.

What we do not do. We do not sell Google user data, do not use it for advertising, do not use it to train AI or machine-learning models, and no person at SnappetAI can read it.

Removing it. "Sign out" in Apps → Cloud & Backup revokes SnappetAI's access and removes the tokens from your iPhone. You can also remove SnappetAI at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Clip copies are files in your own Drive that you can delete there or from the app; the encrypted backup can be removed in Google Drive on the web, under Settings → Manage apps → this app → Delete hidden app data.

SnappetAI's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What the app connects to

SnappetAI has no server of its own. Besides the optional backups above, the app makes these connections:

  • Content files from our public website. Festival lineups, exercise guide photos, the climbing-board climb catalog, and the model used to generate climbs are plain files hosted on GitHub Pages (the same site as this page). Downloading one sends nothing about you or your data. As with any website, the host (GitHub) can see the request's IP address.
  • A product page you ask about. If you use the price check in Wardrobe, the app loads the product link you entered directly from that shop's website, like opening it in a browser. The shop sees an ordinary visit from your IP address; nothing else from the app is sent.
  • Other phones in your household. If you share a household in the Household app, the phones in it exchange that household's chores and activity directly with each other over your local Wi-Fi. Nothing goes through a server.
  • Links you open. Links in the app (for example to a training article) open in your browser, under that site's own privacy policy.

Improving the app

To improve highlight selection, the app records — on your device only — which auto-generated highlights you keep, remove, pin, reorder, or export. This stays in the app's local storage, is used only on-device to tune the selection algorithm for you, and is never transmitted off your device.

Children's privacy

SnappetAI is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them. Because there is no SnappetAI account and no server, we do not receive personal information in the first place.

Data retention and deletion

Because all data is stored locally, deleting the app removes its data from your device. You can also delete individual items within the app, and revoke Health/Photos/Camera/Bluetooth permissions any time in iOS Settings. Copies you chose to keep in your own iCloud or Google Drive stay there until you delete them: turn the backup off in Apps → Cloud & Backup, then remove the copies from iCloud storage settings or from Google Drive as described above.
