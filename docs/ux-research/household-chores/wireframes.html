Household: shared chores, together

A separate mini-app where a household keeps chores on one shared board. Everyone works toward the same goal (cooperative, not a leaderboard). It syncs phone to phone on your home Wi-Fi, with no cloud and no accounts, and works between iPhone and Android.

What these screens decide: (1) what the board looks like day to day, (2) how a household is formed and how devices find each other locally, (3) the cooperative moments: a shared house pet, a house goal, a fair-share view, and help/swap. No XP leaderboard, no "who slacked" list. Chores still feed your personal level through the existing XP rules table, under the same daily cap.

4 · How iPhone ↔ Android talk locally the engineering bet

Multipeer Connectivity is Apple-only and Nearby Share/Quick Share is Android-first, so neither works for both on its own. These do:

A · Bonjour over home Wi-Fi Recommended: P2

mDNS service discovery plus a TCP/TLS socket. Both OSes support it natively: Network.framework on iOS, NsdManager on Android, with no third-party SDK.
  • ✅ Fully cross-platform, standard, well understood
  • ✅ Household = same home network, which is exactly when chores happen
  • ⚠️ Needs both on the same Wi-Fi (guest/mesh networks with client isolation block it)
  • ⚠️ iOS asks for Local Network permission the first time

B · Wi-Fi Aware (NAN) Later: P4

Direct phone-to-phone Wi-Fi, no router. Android 8+ has had it for years; iOS 26 added a public Wi-Fi Aware framework (iPhone 12+) and interop with Android is the stated goal.
  • ✅ Works away from home (holiday cabin, no Wi-Fi)
  • ⚠️ New on iOS, so interop with Android is still being proven in the wild
  • ⚠️ Not every Android phone supports Aware

C · Google Nearby Connections Alternative

One API on both sides (Swift package + Android), built on BLE + Wi-Fi, so it handles discovery and the radio choice for you.
  • ✅ Same mental model on both platforms
  • ⚠️ iOS↔Android discovery is reported as unreliable; the iOS library is less mature
  • ⚠️ A Google dependency in an otherwise first-party app

D · QR "sneakernet" Always-on fallback

When nothing else connects, one phone shows a QR of recent ops and the other scans it. Clunky but bulletproof, and it reuses the camera + QR code we already ship.
  • ✅ Zero networking, works everywhere
  • ⚠️ Manual, and limited in size
The part that matters more than the radio: the data model. Each phone keeps an append-only log of operations (chore created, completed by X at T, reassigned…), each with a unique id and a per-device counter. Syncing means "here's what I've seen, send me what I'm missing". Merging is a pure function of the log, so it's unit-testable with no device, and the transport (A/B/C/D) is just a pipe behind a HouseholdTransport protocol. The same log format is defined once and implemented in both Swift and Kotlin, checked by shared JSON test vectors.
Honest limits of no-cloud: iOS won't keep a socket listening in the background, so syncing happens when the app is open (plus opportunistic background refresh). There are no real-time pushes when you're out. If that turns out to matter, CloudKit can later become a second transport for all-iPhone homes without changing the log.
