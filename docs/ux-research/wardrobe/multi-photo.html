Wardrobe · Multiple photos per item

Device feedback, 2026-08-02: “While adding an entry to closet people should be allowed to upload multiple photos of item: example: front, back, photo of the person wearing it.” This is P2 of the closet feedback trio — P1 (photo pipeline + 1 GB reclaim) is shipped, P3 is the richer add form.

Coral — the one primary CTA Rose — SnappetColor.wardrobe Muted — photo wells
In scope. Up to 6 photos per garment, each with an optional role (Front · Back · Worn · Tag · Detail); add them during capture or later from the item; swipeable carousel on the detail screen; reorder, re-role, set cover, delete. The cover photo stays on WardrobeItem so the closet grid never joins to render a tile — extra photos are separate rows.
Not in scope (P3): brand · size · product link · price paid / current price · custom dropdown values. Not in scope at all: per-photo editing/cropping, video, and using the extra angles as additional AI tagging inputs (the cover alone drafts the tags — see note in step 3).
Flow A
Adding a garment — the photo strip

The existing single-photo flow is untouched up to the review form: pick → cut out → auto-tag. The change is that the review form’s image block becomes a strip. One photo is still a complete, valid garment — everything past the first slot is optional and costs one tap to skip.

Behind the glass
What this costs, and the calls worth arguing with

Storage shape. A new WardrobePhoto row per extra photo (itemID UUID FK, roleRaw, sortIndex, imageData + thumbnailData) — flat table, plain UUID FK, no SwiftData relationships, inline defaults, so it stays CloudKit-compatible like everything else in the module. The cover deliberately stays on WardrobeItem: the closet grid renders 100 tiles and must never join to do it, and it means zero migration — every existing garment is already a valid one-photo item.

Cost at 6 photos. P1 caps each photo at ~1.2 MB master + ~130 KB thumbnail. A fully-photographed garment is therefore ~8 MB rather than the 10 MB a single photo cost before P1. A 100-item closet where every item has 3 photos lands near 390 MB — still a third of what today’s 100 single photos occupied. The 6-photo cap exists for this reason, not for UI tidiness.

Backup. WardrobePhoto needs a mirror Row in SnappetBackup (the testCodecCoversEverySchemaModel tripwire enforces it). Masters ride the backup; thumbnails stay excluded as derived data and regenerate, exactly as P1 established.

Two calls to push back on if you disagree: (1) extra photos don’t feed the AI tagger — a worn shot would pull the dominant-color average toward skin and room, and the cover already produced good tags; (2) roles are a closed set of five here, even though P3 makes other dropdowns extensible, because role drives real behavior (whether to cut out the background) rather than being a label.
