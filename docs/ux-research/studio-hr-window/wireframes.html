Studio — Extended HR Window (per clip)

The idea: a hard move's heart-rate story doesn't end when the camera stops. Each clip's HR chart can now show a wider window than the footage — a configurable lead-in before the clip starts and a tail after it ends — while the live playhead + BPM still track the video exactly across the footage span. Configured per clip in the Studio's HR tool, burned into the export, with honest visual styling for the "off-camera" regions.

1 · The window model

One clip, three regions. HR window = [clip_start − lead, clip_end + tail], clamped to the session's HR coverage. (Your note said clip_end − end_offset — since the goal is to show HR after the video is over, the tail must add past clip_end; modeled that way here.) The chart draws the whole window; the animated dot + live BPM sweep only the footage region, in lockstep with video time.

lead-in — HR before the camera rolled footage — the video; live dot + BPM track here tail — HR after the clip; where a hard move's peak usually lands playhead dot (maps video 0:00→0:28 onto the footage region)

2 · In the Studio editor

The HR tool grows one new section — HR window — under the existing tile design/metrics controls. Two steppers-with-slider (lead, tail), a live mini-map of the window, and a Metrics over scope switch. All per-clip: the values apply to the clip selected on the timeline, and the tile preview updates live (WYSIWYG with export).

3 · How the off-camera regions should read — pick one

The chart must stay honest: viewers should never think the peak happened on camera. Three treatments for the lead/tail regions (all keep the footage region full-strength with thin ember boundary lines):

A · Tinted panes + boundary ticks RECOMMENDED

Lead/tail get a faint zone-tinted wash; footage pane slightly brighter; 1px ember boundaries. Curve stays solid everywhere — the HR data is real, only the camera coverage differs. Cheapest to render in both SwiftUI and the Core-Animation burn (plain CALayer fills).

B · Dimmed curve outside footage

No panes; the curve itself drops to 45% opacity outside the footage. Cleaner look, but on busy video the dimmed tail can disappear — which defeats the feature's whole point (the tail IS the payoff).

C · Dashed off-camera curve

Off-camera segments draw dashed (the export already uses dash for interpolation-sparse windows — this would overload that meaning: dashed = "estimated" today, would also mean "off-camera" tomorrow. Risky semantics).

4 · Defaults, rules & plumbing

RuleBehaviour
DefaultsLead 0:05 (range 0–0:30) · Tail 0:30 (range 0–2:00) — HR typically peaks 10–30s after a max-effort move, so the default tail already captures your use-case with zero configuration. 0 / 0 reproduces today's exact behaviour.
StoragePer-clip on the Studio project: TimelineClip.hrLeadSec / hrTailSec / hrMetricsScope (optional → migration-safe; nil = defaults). Window math extends StudioHRPlacement.captureWindow: [offset + trimStart − lead, offset + trimEnd + tail], still through HRWindowSlicer.
ClampingWindow clamps to the session's HR coverage (no fabricated flat lines past the data). If lead/tail get fully clamped away, the sliders show the effective value (e.g. “0:30 → 0:12 available”).
Playhead mappingfraction = (lead + videoTime·speed⁻¹…) / windowSpan — dot enters at the lead/footage boundary, parks at the footage/tail boundary (non-looping) or wraps (looping preview). Live BPM samples only footage-region time.
Metrics scopeClip only: AVG/PEAK/KCAL/HRV over the footage window (today's per-clip behaviour). Full window (default when tail > 0): over the whole extended window — the tile's PEAK then honestly includes the off-camera 164. The zone chip follows the same scope.
