Festival — dance + video tagging for lineups

End-state wireframes for the Festival mini-app: a Kilter-shaped module that owns the lineup domain (festivals · stages · sets) while capture rides the existing workout spine (dance-discipline sessions, watch HR, Clips, reels). Lineups arrive as downloadable catalog packs from the Snappet web app — the same pattern as the Kilter climb catalog.

festival accent (proposed) — “UV orchid”, dark #D96BE8 / light #B03AC2 brand coral — one primary CTA per surface workout ember — the dance-session spine performance ramp — HR/effort only, never wayfinding
What’s new vs reused. NEW: Festival module (catalog install, schedule, set detail, tag review), pure FestivalSetMatcher (clip-timestamp × set-window overlap), .fpack lineup packs on the web app, ★ plan with pre-set local notifications + clash detection, pure SetRecommender with on-device Foundation-Model reason lines. REUSED: dance WorkoutDiscipline + watch HR capture, session-anchored media assignment (#283), ClipFeedComposer/Filter search & chips, shared ReelView with glass-HR burn, Kilter’s provider→validator→store install pipeline, and the SnappetShareable QR stack (one renderer, one scanner, SharedRoutine’s deflate-blob codec). Later: Clashfinder import, Live Activity for “now playing”.
Flow A · Kilter-style catalog
Get lineups onto the device

Snappet ships no lineup data. Like the Kilter climb catalog, the user brings it on once — Download from Snappet Lineups (hosted packs on the web app) leads, with poster-scan and manual entry as fallbacks — then everything works offline all weekend (festivals famously have no signal).

Flow C · Plan & smart nudges
Star your sets — the phone does the remembering

Starred sets become your plan: local notifications fire a configurable lead before each one (scheduled on-device from the pack’s set times — no network, no push service), clashes are flagged the moment you star two overlapping sets, and a For You sheet suggests what to star next. The ranking is a pure SetRecommender (your HR-per-artist history, gaps in your plan, stage walk time); Apple Intelligence is used narrowly and honestly — on-device Foundation Models write only the one-line why for each suggestion and the poster-scan structuring. Nothing about your taste leaves the phone.

Flow E · Share by QR
Hand a lineup to a friend — no signal needed

The ⋯ menu on the festival schedule (and “Share my plan” on the recap) opens the share sheet. Small payloads — a day plan, a hand-built lineup — are deflate-compressed into the QR itself (snappet://festival/v1/<blob>), so scanning works in a field with zero signal. A whole 412-set festival exceeds the scannable cap, so the sheet automatically falls back to an install-link QR that fetches from music-festivals/. Same decide-by-size logic SharedRoutine ships today.

Flow F · Snappet web app
Lineup packs, hosted like the Kilter catalog

Lineups become a new mini-app on the existing Snappet Pages site — harshal2802.github.io/Snappet/music-festivals/, sibling to board-data/, climb-generator/ and exercise-photos/ (a PR on the Snappet web-app repo adds it). Community-curated .fpack files (plain JSON — festival → days → stages → sets), browsable and downloadable. The phone’s Download from Snappet Lineups hits this host with one user-initiated GET; power users can also AirDrop a pack and use the file-import path, exactly like the Kilter .sqlite3 flow.
