## 1. How does CSI-based sensing work physically, and how does it differ from RSSI-based sensing and from dedicated RF radar (mmWave, UWB)?

### Takeaway
RSSI is one coarse power number per packet; CSI is a per-OFDM-subcarrier amplitude-and-phase measurement of the multipath channel, which is why CSI can pick up small body movements (down to the 5-12 mm chest motion of breathing) while RSSI mainly supports coarse presence/motion. Purpose-built radios (FMCW radar as used by MIT, mmWave, UWB) get range and angle resolution that commodity WiFi lacks, and the most "camera-like" results historically came from those custom radios, not from WiFi chips.

### Cited Findings
- RSSI is a per-packet average, whereas CSI is measured per OFDM subcarrier and carries both amplitude and phase; CSI typically achieves sub-metre indoor localization accuracy versus more than one metre for RSSI, and RSSI is more affected by multipath. — [Survey, PMC9375645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9375645)
- Hardware resolution varies by chipset: the widely used Intel 5300 NIC tool was used for the UT-HAR and Widar datasets, while the Atheros CSI Tool gives finer resolution, 114 subcarriers per antenna pair (NTU-Fi datasets). — [SenseFi, arXiv 2207.07859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07859)
- Breathing is detectable because chest-wall movement changes the reflected ("dynamic") path length by roughly 5-12 mm, which shows up as periodic CSI variation. — [RespirFi, arXiv 2604.20397](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20397v1)
- Fresnel-zone geometry governs sensitivity: CSI respiration sensing works well in the middle of a Fresnel zone and "terribly at the boundaries"; detection worsens as the subject moves toward the edges. — [PMC8157398](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157398)
- Amplitude-only sensing has "blind spots" where respiratory motion is undetectable; CSI amplitude has a cosine dependence and CSI phase a sine dependence on the Fresnel phase, so they are complementary, but raw phase noise on commodity hardware means combining them does not automatically fix blind spots (conjugate multiplication across antennas is one remedy). — [SA-WiSense, arXiv 2507.17623](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17623v2); [WiRM, arXiv 2507.23419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23419)
- Existing respiration methods often require the person to be at a specific location (e.g. within a particular Fresnel zone), which limits deployment flexibility; systems are "highly sensitive to user location". — [RespirFi, arXiv 2604.20397](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20397v1)
- Doppler: WiSee (MobiCom 2013) recognised gestures from the Doppler shifts body motion imprints on WiFi signals, but did so on USRP-N210 software-defined radios, not on a WiFi chipset. — [WiSee paper](https://wisee.cs.washington.edu/)
- Doppler-derived features generalize better than raw CSI: in Widar3.0's cross-environment ablation, raw CSI gave 40.2% and Doppler frequency shift (DFS) profiles 77.8% accuracy, versus 82.6-92.4% for the body-coordinate velocity profile (BVP) derived from multiple links. — [Widar3.0, MobiSys 2019](https://ct.cswu.me/papers/mobisys19_widar3_paper.pdf)
- Beamforming feedback information (BFI) is a different signal from CSI: it is the feedback clients send to the access point for beamforming, and KIT researchers showed it can be used for sensing/identity inference (BFId, ACM CCS 2025). — [KIT press release 069/2025](https://www.kit.edu/kit/english/pi_2025_069_the-spy-who-came-in-from-the-wifi-beware-of-radio-network-surveillance.php); [KIT publication record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000185756)
- CSI access is a practical barrier: it "requires access to low-level WiFi chipset data, which is rarely available on commercial infrastructure" (vendor article; commercial interest noted). — [Terabee](https://www.terabee.com/?p=12660)
- Dedicated radar contrast: MIT's RF-Capture/RF-Pose line uses an FMCW radio (frequency sweep for depth) plus antenna arrays (for direction) over roughly a 5-7 GHz band, i.e. WiFi-like frequencies but not WiFi hardware or waveforms; RF-Pose transmits at about 1000 times lower power than WiFi. — [RF-Capture lecture summary](https://projet.liris.cnrs.fr/origami/topics/gdl/gdl_talk_106/capturewall.pdf); [RF-Pose project page](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/)
- Standardisation: IEEE 802.11bf-2025 ("Enhancements for Wireless LAN Sensing") was approved 28 May 2025 and published 26 September 2025; it covers licence-exempt bands from 1 GHz to 7.125 GHz and above 45 GHz, and adds MAC/PHY procedures for stations to advertise sensing capability, request sensing transmissions and exchange measurement feedback. — [IEEE 802.11bf-2025 front matter](https://people.freebsd.org/~adrian/net80211/80211bf-2025.pdf); [ANSI webstore listing](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ieee/ieee80211bf2025)
- Hardware itself distorts CSI: a 2026 study attributes much cross-device accuracy loss to automatic gain control and subcarrier nonlinearities in receivers, and reports a gain-alignment step recovers up to about 75% of the lost accuracy. — [arXiv 2605.26836](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26836)

### Inferences
- WiFi sensing is fundamentally a bistatic, narrowband (20-160 MHz), few-antenna measurement of how a moving body perturbs multipath. It measures "something changed in the channel" very well and "where/what shape" poorly; dedicated FMCW/UWB/mmWave radars have the bandwidth and arrays to resolve range and angle directly. This physical gap is the root reason "motion detection" is robust and "imaging" is not.
- Because sensitivity depends on Fresnel-zone geometry between a specific transmitter and receiver, coverage is uneven: the same person is easy to detect in one spot and nearly invisible in another, particularly when still.
- 802.11bf standardises how devices ask for and report sensing measurements; it does not by itself improve the physics or accuracy.

### Gaps
- Exact subcarrier counts for each chipset (e.g. Intel 5300 reporting 30 subcarrier groups), typical range-resolution figures (c/2B) for 20/80/160 MHz WiFi vs GHz-bandwidth UWB/mmWave, and the precise RF-Capture band were not confirmed from a primary source in this session.
- No source retrieved gives a direct, like-for-like quantitative comparison of commodity WiFi CSI vs 60 GHz mmWave vs UWB radar on the same sensing task.
- Whether "sensing by proxy" is defined in the published 802.11bf-2025 text was not confirmed.

## 2. What are the landmark works and what did each actually achieve, with what hardware and setup?

### Takeaway
The best-known "see through walls" demonstrations (WiSee 2013, RF-Capture 2015, RF-Pose 2018) used software-defined or custom FMCW radios, not WiFi chips. Commodity-WiFi works (WiFall, Widar3.0, DensePose from WiFi, Person-in-WiFi 3D, WhoFi, LatentCSI) show real capability but in small, controlled setups with dedicated transmitter/receiver pairs and models trained in the same room; 2025-2026 research has shifted to foundation models and generalization rather than new headline capabilities.

### Cited Findings

**Custom / specialised radios (not commodity WiFi)**
- WiSee (Univ. of Washington, MobiCom 2013): classified nine whole-body gestures with 94% average accuracy, including non-line-of-sight and through-wall, on a USRP-N210 software-radio prototype. Accuracy falls to about 60% with a single single-antenna transmitter and degrades as the number of interfering people increases. — [WiSee project page](https://wisee.cs.washington.edu/); [UW Ubicomp Lab](https://ubicomplab.cs.washington.edu/publications/whole-home-gesture/)
- RF-Capture (MIT, SIGGRAPH Asia 2015, "Capturing the Human Figure Through a Wall"): reconstructs a coarse human figure through a wall and tracks limbs in 3D with no body markers, using FMCW plus antenna arrays; distinguished among 15 people with about 88-90% accuracy. — [MIT paper PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/hongzi/content/publications/RFcapture-TOG.pdf); [phys.org coverage](https://phys.org/news/2015-10-wireless-x-ray-vision-power-virtual.pdf)
- RF-Pose (MIT CSAIL, CVPR 2018): 2D skeletons from radio signals, trained with cross-modal supervision from a camera vision model. Average precision 62.4 on visible scenes versus 68.8 for the OpenPose camera system that taught it; 58.1 AP through walls, where the camera baseline fails completely. Uses a low-power FMCW radio, not a WiFi NIC. RF-Pose3D (SIGCOMM 2018) extended this to 3D skeletons with a similar FMCW radio. — [RF-Pose paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mingmin/papers/rfpose-cvpr-zhao.pdf); [project page](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/)

**Commodity WiFi (CSI from off-the-shelf NICs/routers)**
- WiFall (HKUST; IEEE TMC): fall detection from commodity-WiFi CSI. Journal abstract: 90% detection precision with 15% false-alarm rate (one-class SVM) and 94% precision with 13% false alarm (Random Forest); the 2013 thesis and a later survey cite 87% precision / 18% false alarm, in the same environment used for training. — [arXiv 1901.00715 survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00715); [HKUST thesis record](https://researchportal.hkust.edu.hk/en/studentTheses/wifall-device-free-fall-detection-by-wireless-networks/)
- RT-Fall (Peking Univ.): real-time fall detection using CSI amplitude and phase on commodity devices; reported as 91% true-positive rate with 8% false alarm (survey) or 90% sensitivity / 92% specificity (patent text); 13% higher sensitivity and 11% higher specificity than WiFall. — [RT-Fall paper](https://shengjieli-pku.github.io/ShengjieLi/Paper/RT-FALL.pdf); [US patent 10531817](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10531817)
- Widar3.0 (Tsinghua, MobiSys 2019; TPAMI 2022): gesture recognition with 92.7% in-domain accuracy and 82.6-92.4% cross-domain (new environment, location, orientation) without retraining, by deriving a body-coordinate velocity profile from multiple WiFi links. A later independent comparison lists Widar3.0 at 90.48% cross-location, 81.58% cross-orientation, 83.30% cross-environment. — [Widar3.0 paper](https://ct.cswu.me/papers/mobisys19_widar3_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2506.11616](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11616v1)
- DensePose from WiFi (CMU; arXiv 2301.00250, Dec 2022, built on an Aug 2022 CMU master's thesis): maps WiFi signals to dense body-surface (UV) coordinates for multiple people. Under the same-layout protocol WiFi reaches AP 43.5 and AP@50 87.2. Setup: two routers with three antennas each, placed on opposite sides of the subjects, with full control of both units. Authors state performance "is still limited by the public training data ... especially under different layouts"; known difficulties with rare poses and with three or more people. Press noted it was not peer-reviewed at the time of coverage. — [arXiv 2301.00250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00250); [CMU RI thesis](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/dense-human-pose-estimation-from-wifi); [ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2023/01/scientists-find-way-of-using-wifi-to-monitor-people-through-walls.html)
- Person-in-WiFi 3D (Xi'an Jiaotong Univ., CVPR 2024): first end-to-end multi-person 3D pose from WiFi. Joint position error (MPJPE) 91.7 mm for one person, 108.1 mm for two, 125.3 mm for three; proof-of-concept in a 4 m x 3.5 m area, 97K+ frames, seven volunteers. A journal extension reports 93 mm keypoint error and 41 mm for mesh reconstruction. — [CVPR 2024 open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yan_Person-in-WiFi_3D_End-to-End_Multi-Person_3D_Pose_Estimation_with_Wi-Fi_CVPR_2024_paper.html); [XJTU record](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/person-in-wifi-3d-unified-model-for-3d-wifi-perception/)
- WhoFi (Sapienza Univ. of Rome; arXiv 2507.12869, July 2025): person re-identification from CSI with a Transformer; 95.5% Rank-1 accuracy and 88.4% mAP on NTU-Fi, a dataset of only 14 subjects. Academic project with no known deployment plans. — [arXiv 2507.12869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12869); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/22/whofi_wifi_identifier/)
- BFId (KIT/KASTEL, ACM CCS, Oct 2025): identity inference from beamforming feedback information on a new dataset of 197 individuals; paper reports 99.5% +/- 0.38 accuracy; KIT says identification worked "independently of the perspective or their gait". Dataset is access-restricted. — [KIT press release](https://www.kit.edu/kit/english/pi_2025_069_the-spy-who-came-in-from-the-wifi-beware-of-radio-network-surveillance.php); [KIT publication record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000185756)

**2024-2026: generative and foundation-model approaches**
- LatentCSI (Institute of Science Tokyo; arXiv 2506.10605, 2025): generates high-resolution images of a scene from WiFi CSI by mapping CSI into the latent space of a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 3 per press), with optional text guidance; validated on a self-collected wide-band CSI dataset and a subset of MM-Fi. The model is trained on real photos of the same room. Earlier CSI-to-image work used GANs (CSI2Image, 2021). — [arXiv 2506.10605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10605); [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/wi-fi-signals-can-now-create-accurate-images-of-a-room-with-the-help-of-pre-trained-ai-latentcsi-leverages-stable-diffusion-3-to-turn-wi-fi-data-into-a-digital-paintbrush)
- "Scale What Counts, Mask What Matters" (arXiv 2511.18792, Nov 2025): masked-autoencoder pretraining on 1.3M+ CSI samples from 14 datasets, 4 device types, 2.4/5/6 GHz. Pretraining improves cross-domain accuracy by 2.2% to 15.7% over supervised baselines; larger models give only marginal gains, so "data, rather than model capacity, is the current bottleneck". — [arXiv 2511.18792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18792v1)
- UniCSI (arXiv 2610.09559, 2026): carrier-frequency- and bandwidth-agnostic CSI encoder with JEPA pretraining; authors claim fixed-grid architectures "collapse toward chance" on hard cross-domain benchmarks while UniCSI transfers. — [arXiv 2610.09559](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09559)
- "The Universal Language of CSI" (arXiv 2607.09727, 2026): treats CSI as a language with a learnable grammar across heterogeneous datasets; authors report strong generalization to new environments and few-shot efficiency. — [arXiv 2607.09727](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09727)
- WiSPER (arXiv 2610.07025, 2026): flow-refinement method for multi-person 3D pose from CSI, evaluated on the Person-in-WiFi 3D data. — [arXiv 2610.07025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07025)
- Diffusion models are also used to synthesise CSI training data (data augmentation) rather than to image people. — [arXiv 2404.04829](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04829v1); [Auburn TCCN paper](https://eng.auburn.edu/~szm0001/papers/TCCN-GAI-24-0089_final.pdf)
- Vendor-scale deployment evidence: an "experience paper" on scaling WiFi sensing to millions of commodity home devices reports 92.61% overall accuracy and cutting false alarms from non-human movement from 63.1% to 8.4%. — [arXiv 2506.04322](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04322v1)

### Inferences
- A consistent pattern: the more camera-like the output (figure, skeleton, dense pose, image), the more specialised the radio or the more controlled the room. The famous MIT through-wall skeleton videos are radar results operating at WiFi-like frequencies, and are routinely misattributed to "WiFi".
- Commodity-WiFi pose works need (a) dedicated, cooperating transmitter and receiver with CSI extraction firmware, (b) multiple antennas, (c) camera-labelled training data from the same space. None of them works from an unmodified home router plus a phone.
- The 2025-2026 foundation-model papers are an admission that generalization, not capability in a fixed lab, is the unsolved problem; gains reported so far (2-16 points) are incremental. The 2026 arXiv preprints were seen only as abstracts and should be treated as author claims.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved in this session: the original Person-in-WiFi (ICCV 2019), WiTrack, Wi-Vi (2013), E-eyes, CARM, WiGait, RF-Avatar details, and DensePose-from-WiFi's exact different-layout AP figures and NIC model. The DensePose paper's different-layout results table exists (CMU thesis) but the values were not visible; the size of that drop is the single most relevant number that remains unconfirmed.
- Whether DensePose from WiFi was ever published in a peer-reviewed venue was not confirmed.
- No independent replication results were found for WhoFi, BFId or LatentCSI.
- arxiv.org must be added to the session's allowed network domains to verify any of the above from full text.

## 3. How realistic is "camera-like" imaging or pose estimation with commodity WiFi versus simple motion/presence detection? What are the known failure modes?

### Takeaway
Motion and presence detection is mature enough to ship on millions of commodity devices; camera-like pose or imaging remains a same-room laboratory result whose apparent visual detail comes largely from a model trained on photos of that room. The dominant failure mode across the literature is domain shift (new room, person, device, placement), followed by non-human motion (pets, robot vacuums, oscillating appliances), multiple people, and stationary people.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials survey reviewing 200+ papers since 2015 frames generalization as the core problem: systems often fail on new users, devices or rooms because hardware differences and body/environment variation change the measured CSI/RSSI. — [arXiv 2503.08008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08008v4)
- Concrete cross-environment collapse: in a 2024 practicality study, two CSI machine-learning models above 85% accuracy in the original setting fell to roughly 30% in a different environment. — [arXiv 2409.07495](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07495v1)
- Raw-CSI learning transfers worst: 40.2% cross-environment accuracy for raw CSI in Widar3.0's ablation versus 92.7% in-domain for the full system. — [Widar3.0 paper](https://ct.cswu.me/papers/mobisys19_widar3_paper.pdf)
- In-domain numbers can be near-perfect and therefore misleading: a 2025 paper reports 99.72% in-domain accuracy on Widar3. — [arXiv 2512.04521](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04521v1)
- Mitigations help but do not close the gap: ReWiS (few-shot) reports under a 10% accuracy drop across environments; GenFi uses feature disentanglement and meta-learning for unseen rooms/people; foundation-model pretraining adds 2.2-15.7 points cross-domain. — [ReWiS, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10356846); [GenFi, ICC 2025](https://www.ece.nus.edu.sg/stfpage/bsikdar/papers/icc_han_25.pdf); [arXiv 2511.18792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18792v1)
- Multiple people degrade results: Person-in-WiFi 3D error rises from 91.7 mm (1 person) to 125.3 mm (3 people); DensePose from WiFi struggles with three or more people and with rare poses; WiSee accuracy drops as interfering users increase. — [CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yan_Person-in-WiFi_3D_End-to-End_Multi-Person_3D_Pose_Estimation_with_Wi-Fi_CVPR_2024_paper.html); [ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2023/01/scientists-find-way-of-using-wifi-to-monitor-people-through-walls.html); [WiSee paper](https://wisee.cs.washington.edu/)
- Pose accuracy scale: best reported WiFi 3D joint errors are around 9-13 cm in a 4 m x 3.5 m test area with seven volunteers. — [CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yan_Person-in-WiFi_3D_End-to-End_Multi-Person_3D_Pose_Estimation_with_Wi-Fi_CVPR_2024_paper.html)
- Deployment constraints for pose: DensePose from WiFi needs two 3-antenna routers on opposite sides of the target with full control of both; "the weakness of WiFi signals also limits the range and accuracy is still an issue". — [ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2023/01/scientists-find-way-of-using-wifi-to-monitor-people-through-walls.html)
- "Imaging" caveat: LatentCSI's photorealistic output comes from a pretrained diffusion model trained with real photos of the room; CSI steers the generation. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/wi-fi-signals-can-now-create-accurate-images-of-a-room-with-the-help-of-pre-trained-ai-latentcsi-leverages-stable-diffusion-3-to-turn-wi-fi-data-into-a-digital-paintbrush); [arXiv 2506.10605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10605)
- Non-human motion: pets trigger false positives in deployed home systems, as do robotic vacuums and oscillating appliances; before mitigation, 63.1% false-alarm rate from non-human movement in one large-scale deployment paper, reduced to 8.4%. Origin Wireless slides likewise name pets and household appliances as a major false-alarm source. — [arXiv 2506.04322](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04322v1); [WiSense 2025 slides](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/WiSense25/Guozhen_Zhu.pdf)
- Stationary people: sensing a still person relies on breathing (5-12 mm path change) and suffers location-dependent blind spots. — [RespirFi](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20397v1); [SA-WiSense](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17623v2)
- Occupancy counting: one CSI study with up to 30 people reported about 80% of estimates within one person indoors and about 70% within two outdoors; device placement near walls changed stationary-crowd counting accuracy by 11.2%. A vendor (competitor) article claims WiFi tracks the shape of occupancy trends but diverges in absolute numbers, with "~30-50% error in real-world conditions" for RSSI methods and models that are "environment-specific and fragile, requiring recalibration". — [arXiv 1901.00715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.00715); [PolyU paper](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/116211/1/Wang_Wall_Proximity_Matters.pdf); [Terabee (vendor, treat with caution)](https://www.terabee.com/?p=12660)
- Identification: gait/body-signature ID reaches 95.5% Rank-1 among 14 people (WhoFi, CSI) and 99.5% among 197 (BFId, BFI); RF-Capture's custom radar got about 88% among 15. — [arXiv 2507.12869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12869); [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000185756); [MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/111588?show=full)

### Inferences
- Realism ranking for a home-security product on commodity WiFi (my synthesis): whole-home motion detection (deployable now) > human-vs-pet discrimination and room-level localisation (works with engineering effort, non-trivial false alarms) > breathing/still-presence (works at favourable positions) > fall detection (false-alarm rates of 8-18% in papers are too high for unattended alarms) > occupancy count (trend-level) > identification (strong closed-set lab results, unproven in the wild) > pose/"imaging" (lab only).
- "WiFi as a camera" is not an accurate description of what a consumer product can do. No retrieved study shows recognisable imagery or pose from an unmodified router in a room the model has never seen.
- Closed-set identification results (pick 1 of N enrolled people) do not show the ability to flag an unknown intruder as "not a household member"; that open-set problem is harder and the retrieved works did not report it.
- Fans specifically: the general mechanism (periodic motion of a reflector) makes them a plausible false-alarm source, and deployed-system papers name "oscillating appliances", but no quantified fan study was found.

### Gaps
- No quantified study isolating ceiling/desk fans, curtains moving in airflow, or motion outside the home (neighbours, passing cars) as false-alarm sources was retrieved.
- No independent (non-author, non-vendor) field trial of WiFi sensing in real homes over months was found.
- The specific cross-domain accuracy figures tabulated in the 2026 generalization survey could not be extracted (full text blocked).
- Whether LatentCSI can depict a person the model never saw in training, or a different room, was not verified from the paper.

## 4. What accuracy/false-alarm numbers are reported for intrusion/presence detection specifically, including through walls?

### Takeaway
Self-reported figures for CSI-based intrusion/motion detection cluster at 96-99.7% detection with 0-3% false alarms, but these come from the authors' own test setups (often a handful of environments, often without pets), and the one large-scale deployment paper shows that untreated non-human motion can dominate false alarms. No independent benchmark of intrusion detection exists in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- WiBorder (Peking Univ.): 99.4% detection rate and 0.68% false-alarm rate "in real-life scenarios"; its goal is boundary-precise sensing, i.e. confining detection to a room bounded by walls rather than triggering on motion beyond them. — [WiBorder paper](https://shengjieli-pku.github.io/ShengjieLi/Paper/WiBorder.pdf)
- WiDetect (Origin Wireless/Univ. of Maryland, UbiComp 2019): whole-home motion detector (not a human-vs-other classifier) claiming 99.68% detection accuracy with zero false rate. — [WiDetect paper](https://ct.cswu.me/papers/ubicomp19_widetect_paper.pdf)
- Origin Wireless (WiSense 2025 workshop slides): average intrusion detection rate 96.67% with average false-alarm rate 1.92%; pets and household appliances identified as a major false-alarm source. — [Slides](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/WiSense25/Guozhen_Zhu.pdf)
- Large-scale commodity deployment ("millions of devices" experience paper, 2025): 92.61% overall accuracy; false alarms from non-human movement reduced from 63.1% to 8.4%. — [arXiv 2506.04322](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04322v1)
- GuardFi-style approach (patent text, Origin-affiliated): two-stage pipeline, detect motion then classify human vs non-human; claims high detection and low false alarm in unseen environments "with model re-tuning" (no number in the excerpt). — [US patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12352889)
- Through-wall with a WiFi illuminator (older localisation work): 96.92% detection with under 3.08% false alarm in both line-of-sight and non-line-of-sight. — [Sensors 2021, doi 10.3390/s21072287](https://www.doi.org/10.3390/S21072287)
- Through-wall on software radios: WiSee's 94% gesture accuracy held in non-line-of-sight and through-wall scenarios (USRP hardware). — [WiSee](https://wisee.cs.washington.edu/)
- Through-wall on custom radar: RF-Pose 58.1 AP through a wall versus 62.4 AP in line of sight. — [RF-Pose paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mingmin/papers/rfpose-cvpr-zhao.pdf)
- A 2026 IEEE Wireless Communications Letters preprint argues existing intrusion schemes destroy CSI structure through lossy preprocessing, "causing high false alarm rates and poor generalization". — [Southampton preprint](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~sqc/listP/IEEEWCLwifiS2026.pdf)
- RSSI-only approach exists as a low-cost alternative for presence and people counting. — [arXiv 2308.06773](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.06773)
- WiFi sensing is weaker in non-line-of-sight conditions (lower localisation accuracy). — [Staffordshire Univ. survey](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/6035/1/JOE.2019.0790.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 1-2% false-alarm rate per test trial is not the same as an acceptable false-alarm rate per day for a security product. A motion detector evaluated continuously can still generate several alerts per week at such rates; the papers' denominators (per window, per event, per hour) differ and were not visible in snippets.
- Through-wall sensitivity cuts both ways for security: the same penetration that enables whole-home coverage from one link also picks up motion outside the protected area (the problem WiBorder exists to solve).
- The 63.1% to 8.4% figure is the most deployment-realistic number found; it indicates that even after mitigation, roughly one in twelve non-human motion events is still misclassified in the authors' own evaluation.

### Gaps
- Per-day or per-month false-alarm rates in real occupied homes, the number of homes tested, and test duration were not obtainable (slides and arXiv full text blocked).
- No head-to-head comparison of WiFi sensing against PIR motion sensors or cameras for intrusion detection was retrieved.
- No data on detection of a deliberately slow or crawling intruder, or on adversarial evasion/jamming, was retrieved.
- The WiDetect "zero false rate" claim's test conditions (duration, presence of pets/appliances) were not verified.

## 5. What public datasets and benchmarks exist?

### Takeaway
Public datasets are small (7 to 40 people, one to a few rooms) relative to vision datasets, which is itself the main cause of the generalization problem; SenseFi is the standard model benchmark and MM-Fi the main multimodal pose dataset, with 2025-2026 efforts pooling a dozen-plus datasets for pretraining.

### Cited Findings
- SenseFi (NTU Singapore / Univ. of Edinburgh; arXiv 2207.07859, 2022): open library and benchmark evaluating deep models (MLP, CNN, RNN, Transformer families) under supervised, transfer and unsupervised learning on four datasets: UT-HAR, Widar, NTU-Fi HAR, NTU-Fi Human-ID. Code is public. — [arXiv 2207.07859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07859); [GitHub](https://github.com/xyanchen/WiFi-CSI-Sensing-Benchmark)
- UT-HAR: activity recognition, 7 classes, Intel 5300 NIC; 3,977 training / 996 test samples in the SenseFi split. — [GitHub](https://github.com/xyanchen/WiFi-CSI-Sensing-Benchmark)
- Widar (Widar3.0 data, Tsinghua): largest WiFi gesture dataset, 22 gesture categories, Intel 5300; about 43K samples (paper) or about 34.9K train / 8.7K test (repo split). — [arXiv 2207.07859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07859); [GitHub](https://github.com/xyanchen/WiFi-CSI-Sensing-Benchmark)
- NTU-Fi HAR and NTU-Fi Human-ID: Atheros CSI Tool, 114 subcarriers per antenna pair; Human-ID is gait-based identification of 14 subjects walking with different clothing/backpack. — [arXiv 2207.07859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07859); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/22/whofi_wifi_identifier/)
- MM-Fi (NTU; NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks): 320K+ synchronised frames, 40 subjects, 27 actions (14 daily, 13 rehabilitation), 1,080 sequences; modalities RGB, depth, LiDAR (Ouster OS1), mmWave radar (TI IWR6843AOP), WiFi CSI (commodity TP-Link N750 routers with Atheros CSI tooling); annotations include 2D/3D pose, action, 3D position, dense pose. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3baf7a39d07e9f4f1e258a412df94521-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf); [arXiv 2305.10345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10345v2)
- Person-in-WiFi 3D dataset: 97K+ frames, 7 volunteers, 4 m x 3.5 m area, up to three people, Kinect-derived labels; 89,946 train / 7,824 test. — [XJTU AIRS lab page](https://airslab2020.github.io/labpages/papers/person-in-wifi-3d-end-to-end-multi-person-3d-pose-estimation-with-wi-fi/index.html); [project page](https://aiotgroup.github.io/Person-in-WiFi-3D)
- WiMANS: multi-user activity dataset (referenced as the source of a dataset comparison table). — [arXiv 2402.09430](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09430)
- BFId dataset: BFI recordings of 197 individuals, access-restricted via KIT agreement. — [B2FIND record](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/e524f533-9a2f-55e9-8886-f9d8abe3f687); [KIT agreement](https://ps.tm.kit.edu/downloads/BFId-dataset-agreement.pdf)
- Pooled pretraining corpus: 14 datasets, 1.3M+ samples, 4 device types, 2.4/5/6 GHz (arXiv 2511.18792). — [arXiv 2511.18792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18792v1)
- The 2026 generalization survey includes a catalogue of datasets as part of its taxonomy. — [arXiv 2503.08008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08008)

### Inferences
- None of the benchmark datasets found targets home intrusion (empty home vs intruder vs pet vs appliance, across many homes). The benchmarks measure gesture/activity classification in labs, so benchmark accuracy says little about security false-alarm performance.
- With the largest pose dataset at 40 subjects and the largest pooled corpus at 1.3M samples, WiFi sensing data is orders of magnitude smaller than vision data; "foundation model" is aspirational at this scale, consistent with the finding that data is the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No public intrusion/presence dataset with pets and appliances across multiple real homes was found.
- Subject and room counts for UT-HAR and Widar3.0, and details of other datasets (SignFi, CSI-Bench, XRF55, WiMANS statistics), were not retrieved.
- No leaderboard-style, standardised cross-domain benchmark with agreed protocols was confirmed; papers use differing splits (e.g. Widar3.0 numbers differ between the original paper and later comparisons).
